# Google Ads

Three tools in one skill: the **competitor ad finder**, the **ad writer** and the **keyword tool**. Start with the website. If the person also drops in a Google Ads report, use it to rewrite what is live.

## 0. Start

Open the website the person gives you. Write down in one short block: what they sell, to whom, where, the price level, and what makes them different. Ask one question only if something important is missing (for example the area they serve).

## 1. Competitor ad finder

1. Find 3 to 5 real competitors: search the web for the main service plus the area, and take the businesses that show up with ads or at the top.
2. For each competitor, look them up in the Google Ads Transparency Center (adstransparency.google.com): search the company name or website and filter to search ads where possible. If you can browse it, record their current search ads: headlines, descriptions, the offer, and the landing page they send people to.
3. If you cannot open the Transparency Center from here, say so plainly and ask the person to search their competitors there and paste the ads or screenshots. Never invent ads.
4. Sum up the patterns: the offers that repeat, the words that repeat, the promises everyone makes, and the gap nobody covers.

The Transparency Center shows ads, not keywords or budgets. Never claim to know a competitor's keywords or spend.

## 2. Read their own report (if they drop one in)

Accept a CSV or Excel export from Google Ads (campaigns, ad groups, ads, keywords or search terms, with cost, clicks, conversions). Then:

- list what works: the ads, keywords and search terms with conversions at a fair cost;
- list what wastes money: cost with no conversions, search terms that do not fit (these become negatives);
- say where to move the budget, in plain numbers ("move about 20% from campaign A to campaign B because...").

If there is no report, skip this step and say what to export next time.

## 3. Ad writer

Write responsive search ads for each main service:

- 15 headlines, each 30 characters or fewer (count them), including the service, the area, the offer, proof and a call to action;
- 4 descriptions, each 90 characters or fewer;
- one landing page note: what the first screen of the page must say so it matches the ad.

Base them on what works in their report and on the gap from the competitor ads. Mark which headlines to pin, if any. Never make claims the business cannot back up.

## 4. Keyword tool

Build a keyword plan for each service:

- ad groups by intent (urgent, price, comparison, local);
- 10 to 20 keywords per group, phrase and exact match;
- a negative keyword list (jobs, free, DIY, training, wrong areas, and anything wasteful from the report);
- a note on which groups to start with on a small budget.

You cannot see Google's search volumes from here unless the person pastes Keyword Planner data. Say so instead of guessing numbers.

## 5. Hand over

Finish with: the top three changes to make this week, then the ads, then the keywords, each in a clean block they can copy.

## Rules

- Never invent competitor ads, volumes, prices or results.
- Character limits are strict: check every headline and description.
- Plain words. No jargon unless you explain it in the same sentence.
