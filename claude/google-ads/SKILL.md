# Google Ads

Three tools in one skill: the **Ad Library Scraper**, the **Ad Writer** and the **Keyword Tool**. Start with the website. If the person's own Google Ads account is connected (or they drop in a report), use it to rewrite what is live.

## 0. Start

The website is what the person typed after `/google-ads`. If there is none, ask for it in one line and wait.

Open the website. Write down in one short block: what they sell, to whom, where, the price level, and what makes them different. Ask one question only if something important is missing (for example the area they serve).

## 1. Ad Library Scraper

1. Find 3 to 5 real competitors: search the web for the main service plus the area, and take the businesses that show up with ads or at the top.
2. For each competitor, open the Google Ads Transparency Center (adstransparency.google.com), search the company name or website, and filter to search ads. Record every current search ad you can see: headlines, descriptions, the offer, and the landing page it sends people to. Open the landing pages and note what the first screen says.
3. The Transparency Center is a live web app. If you have a browser tool, use it. If you cannot open it from here, say so plainly and ask the person to search their competitors there and paste the ads or screenshots. Never invent ads.
4. From their ads and landing pages, list the search words each competitor is clearly going after. Say plainly that this is read from their ads: Google does not publish anyone's bid list or budget.
5. Sum up the patterns: the offers that repeat, the words that repeat, the promises everyone makes, and the gap nobody covers.

## 2. Their own account

**If a Google Ads connection is available** (for example Google's official Google Ads MCP server, which is read-only): list their accounts, ask which one, then pull the last 30 days, or 90 if spend is small: campaigns, ad groups, ads, keywords and search terms with cost, clicks and conversions.

**If not**, ask them to export a report from Google Ads (CSV or Excel with campaigns, ads, keywords or search terms, with cost, clicks, conversions) and drop it in. If they have neither, skip this step and say what to connect or export next time.

Then:

- list what works: the ads, keywords and search terms with conversions at a fair cost;
- list what wastes money: cost with no conversions, search terms that do not fit (these become negatives);
- say where to move the budget, in plain numbers ("move about 20% from campaign A to campaign B because...").

You only read the account. Never change anything in it: the person makes the changes.

## 3. Ad Writer

Rewrite their ads, or write new ones, for each main service:

- 15 headlines, each 30 characters or fewer (count them), including the service, the area, the offer, proof and a call to action;
- 4 descriptions, each 90 characters or fewer;
- one landing page note: what the first screen of the page must say so it matches the ad.

Base them on what already works in their account and on the gap in the competitor ads. Mark which headlines to pin, if any. Never make claims the business cannot back up.

## 4. Keyword Tool

Build a keyword plan for each service:

- ad groups by intent (urgent, price, comparison, local);
- 10 to 20 keywords per group, phrase and exact match;
- a negative keyword list (jobs, free, DIY, training, wrong areas, and anything wasteful from their account);
- a note on which groups to start with on a small budget.

You cannot see Google's search volumes from here unless the person pastes Keyword Planner data. Say so instead of guessing numbers.

## 5. Hand over

Finish with: the top three changes to make this week, then the ads, then the keywords, each in a clean block they can copy.

## Rules

- Never invent competitor ads, keywords, volumes, prices or results.
- Character limits are strict: check every headline and description.
- Plain words. No jargon unless you explain it in the same sentence.
