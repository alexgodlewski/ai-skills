# Call Prep

A lead books a call, then waits. In that gap they Google you, doubt the price, and wonder if it works for them. If nothing answers those doubts, they stay quiet, and many never show. This skill plans what they see in the gap. The core is a content plan. Ads are only one way to deliver it.

You do not write the confirmation page or the email sequence. Other skills do those. You plan the content and the DMs, and you can point to a page or an email as one delivery spot.

## 1. Ask for the inputs

Ask for all of this in ONE message, then wait:

- **The offer:** what they sell, who it is for, the price level, how it is delivered.
- **How calls get booked:** DMs by hand, a setter, an AI setter, a funnel or ads. Where does the lead go to book (link or calendar)?
- **The top doubts and questions** buyers have before they buy. Ask for 5 to 10. A good source is what the person or their closer answers on every call. If they have no list, offer to draft one for them to confirm. Mark any doubt you add yourself as a guess.
- **What content they already have:** Reels, posts, testimonials, case studies, a FAQ, a long video or podcast. Ask for links or short notes about what each one covers.
- **How many days** usually pass between booking and the call. If it varies, ask for the common case.
- **Do they run ads?** Yes or no.
- **A sample of their writing** (a few DMs or captions), so the scripts sound like them.

Never invent their results, testimonials, numbers or client names. If something is missing, ask.

## 2. Sort the doubts

Put each doubt in one of four groups:

- **Question:** a basic thing they want to know (how it works, who it is for, how it differs from other options).
- **Follow-up question:** what they will ask right after the first answer.
- **Objection:** price, time, "will it work for me", "I tried something like this before".
- **Expectation:** what the process looks like, how long it takes, what the person must do.

Check that every group has at least one doubt. If a group is empty, ask the person or add a clearly marked guess.

## 3. Build the call prep map

Make a table. One row per doubt. Columns:

| Doubt | Group | Content that answers it | Have it or make it | Where the lead sees it | Days before the call |

- **Content that answers it:** name one piece the person already has, with the link or title they gave you. Match on what the piece really covers. If nothing fits, write "make new".
- **Where the lead sees it:** pick ONE from this list: a DM with a link, a Story, a retargeting ad to people who booked, or an email. Do not use a channel the person does not use.
- **Days before the call:** spread the rows so the lead gets a little each day, not everything at once. Put the biggest objection early, and the expectations piece close to the call. Use the real gap the person gave you. If the gap is short, show fewer rows and say so.

Under the table, list the gaps: how many doubts have no content yet. Pick the 3 to 5 that matter most for the missing pieces.

## 4. Write the 3 to 5 missing scripts

For each missing piece, write a short spoken script the person can film on a phone. Keep each one under about 60 seconds when read out loud.

Format for each script:

- **Doubt it answers**
- **Script:** line one names the doubt in the buyer's own words. The middle answers it with a plain reason or a short story. The end gives one clear takeaway.
- **Proof slot:** where a real example goes, written like `[your client's result here]`. Tell the person what to put there.

Rules for scripts: no call to action, no "book now". The piece should help even if the person never buys. Do not use music with copyright if they will also run it as an ad. Say that once in a note.

## 5. Write the DM messages

Write the messages that deliver the content. Cover:

- **The first DM after they book:** thank them, say when the call is, and say you will send a few short things that answer common questions.
- **One DM per content piece:** a line on why it is useful for them, plus the link or the video. One idea per message.
- **A short reminder DM** the day before the call, with the time and the link, and nothing else.
- **A reply for the lead who goes quiet:** friendly, no pressure, easy to say "I need to move it".

Only message people who are already in a conversation with the person, or who gave their contact details when they booked. Never write messages for people who did not agree to be contacted.

## 6. Retargeting setup (only if they run ads)

Explain it in plain words, in a few steps:

1. Make a **custom audience** of people who booked a call. The ad platform's tool for this is usually called a custom audience, built from your booking list or a booking event. Use only data people gave you for this purpose, and follow the platform's rules and local privacy law.
2. Run **one video ad per content piece** to that audience only. Use video views as the goal, not clicks.
3. **Stop showing a piece to people who already watched it,** so each lead sees different pieces and not the same one again.
4. Set a **small daily budget.** The person picks the number. Do not suggest a number or promise a result. Tell them to watch it for a few days and change it only if the ads are not reaching the booked leads.
5. Check that the audience is big enough for the platform to show ads. If the person books only a few calls a week, say plainly that retargeting may not run, and DMs and Stories can do the job.

No tricks to get around ad rules, and no fake countdowns or "only 2 spots left" lines anywhere in the plan.

## Rules for every message and script

- Sound like a real person. Short sentences, plain words, a 6th grade reading level.
- No dashes used as punctuation. No hype words.
- Match the person's writing sample if they gave one.
- Never invent proof. Use `[slots]` and say what goes in them.

## What to do now

1. Film the 3 to 5 missing scripts this week. One take on a phone is fine.
2. Save the DM messages as templates, so you can paste one each day.
3. Send the first message the moment someone books, and the rest on the days in the map.
4. If you run ads, set up the booked audience and one ad per piece, with a daily budget you choose.
5. Ask your closer which questions still come up on calls, and add them to the map next month.

**Measure:** the show rate before and after, plus how often the closer says "I already answered that for them."
