# The No Map

Six kinds of no, and what to write back to each one. A free guide by [Alex Godlewski](https://www.instagram.com/alexgodlewsky/).

## Every no gets a name first

Forty six thousand, seven hundred and eighteen people said no in the messages, before anybody got on a call. We sorted all of them. Nine out of ten fit into six groups.

Your team answers all six the same way. Only one of them means no.

Name the no first. Then send the message for that name.

## Out of a hundred who books

| The no | Out of 100 who book | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Busy** | 15 | Fix the time. |
| **Price** | 12 | At once, or not worth it? |
| **Fear** | 10 | Ask what happened last time. |
| **Trust** | 7 | Answer their question first. |
| **Later** | 6 | Same day, then a date. |
| **Real** | less than 1 | Let it go. |

## Busy (15 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "I can't right now." "This week is crazy." "I can't make it."

Send this, word for word:

> Is it just hard to find a time, or do you want to leave this for now?

If it is the time:

> Makes sense. I have 9:15 tonight, or 7 tomorrow morning. Which one is easier?

Two times, no link, one of them in the evening. If they want to leave it, go to Later.

## Price (12 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "It's too expensive." "I can't afford it."

Send this:

> Fair. Is it too much to pay at once, or are you not sure it's worth it?

Too much at once: talk about smaller payments. Not sure: the price stays, go back to the thing they told you they want. If they do not know the price yet, tell them the number. Nobody on your team cuts the price first.

## Fear (10 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "I don't think this will work for me."

Send this:

> What happened the last time you tried?

They tell you why it went wrong. Send one story back: a client who failed in the same way, and what was different the second time. Do not send your best client. A big win makes this person feel too small.

## Trust (7 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "Who is this?" "Is this a bot?" "Why are you asking me so many questions?"

If it is AI, say it:

> Yes, I'm Anna's AI assistant. I asked about your goal so I don't send you something you can't use. Want to see an example?

If it is a person, say it:

> I'm Tom, I work with Anna. I asked about your goal so I don't send you something you can't use. Want to see an example?

Answer first. Ask later.

## Later (6 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "Maybe next month." "I have to think." "I'll get back to you."

The same day:

> No problem. What has to happen first?

Then get a date:

> Got it. Can I write to you on the third?

On that day:

> You told me the third would be better. Is it?

Never write: just checking in.

## Real (less than 1 of 100 book)

Sounds like: "I don't really need this." "I'll try it on my own first." "I already have someone."

Send this, then leave:

> Got it, thanks for telling me. I'll leave it here. If that changes, just write to me.

Do not ask why. Do not make a last offer. This is the one no that means no.

## Do this tonight

1. **Read your last 50 no's.** Write one of the six names next to each.
2. **Count the names.** The biggest one is the first message to fix.
3. **No name fits?** Then you answer that person, not your team.
4. **Send this page to your team.** To everybody who writes to a lead.
