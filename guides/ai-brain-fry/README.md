# 4 Rules for Using AI Without Frying Your Brain

A free guide by [Alex Godlewski](https://www.instagram.com/alexgodlewsky/). It has my four rules and every study from the video, with the links so you can read them yourself.

**One-page PDF:** [ai-brain-fry.pdf](ai-brain-fry.pdf)

## The short version

AI makes each task faster. But many people fill that saved time with more hard work, watch the AI run all day, and use it on their breaks. That is where the tired, foggy feeling comes from. These four rules fix it.

## The 4 rules

### 1. Do not fill the saved time with more hard work

If AI saves you an hour, do not hand that hour to another hard task. Put easy, low-effort tasks back in on purpose: take a walk, do the dishes, make lunch. Your brain needs the slow time to recover.

### 2. Use no more than three AI tools at a time

Pick the three you really need and drop the rest. In the BCG survey below, people said they got more done when they went from one tool to two to three. After three, the scores went down.

### 3. Stop watching the AI work

Do not sit and stare while it runs. Send the task out, go do something else, and come back later. Then review the results in one batch instead of checking every few minutes. Watching a machine work is tiring, and it does not make the work better.

### 4. Protect your breaks

No "one more prompt" at lunch. No "one more prompt" at the end of the day. Set a time when you stop, and stop. The Berkeley study below found that people started squeezing small AI tasks into the moments that used to be breaks.

## The studies

### 1. "AI brain fry": Harvard Business Review, March 2026

Researchers from Boston Consulting Group (BCG) and the University of California, Riverside surveyed 1,488 full-time workers in the US, in January 2026. They called the feeling "AI brain fry": mental tiredness from using or watching AI too much.

What they reported:

- 14% of the workers said they had it.
- It was most common in marketing, at about one in four (25% to 26%, depending on the write-up).
- Productivity scores went up from one tool to two to three tools, then dipped after three.
- Workers with brain fry scored 11% higher on minor errors and 39% higher on major errors.
- Intent to quit was 34% for workers with brain fry, against 25% for workers without it.
- Burnout scores were 15% lower when AI took over routine, repetitive work.

Read it: [When Using AI Leads to "Brain Fry" (HBR)](https://hbr.org/2026/03/when-using-ai-leads-to-brain-fry)
BCG summary: [bcg.com](https://www.bcg.com/news/5march2026-when-using-ai-leads-brain-fry)
Where the numbers above come from, since HBR is behind a paywall: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/marketers-report-highest-rates-of-ai-brain-fry-report-finds)

Note: the authors call these findings early. It is one survey, taken once.

### 2. "AI Doesn't Reduce Work, It Intensifies It": UC Berkeley Haas, February 2026

Professor Aruna Ranganathan and doctoral student Xingqi Maggie Ye spent eight months inside a US tech company of about 200 people. They watched the work on site, sat in meetings, and ran more than 40 interviews.

What they found: with AI, people worked faster, took on more kinds of tasks, and worked more hours of the day. Workers sent prompts at lunch, before meetings and in the evening, so breaks got blurry. Some kept many AI threads running at once.

Read it: [AI Doesn't Reduce Work, It Intensifies It (HBR)](https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it)
Summary from Berkeley: [Haas newsroom](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/ai-promised-to-free-up-workers-time-uc-berkeley-haas-researchers-found-the-opposite/)

Note: this is one company, so it may not be true everywhere.

### 3. "Ironies of Automation": Lisanne Bainbridge, 1983

A short, famous paper from 1983. Its main point: when a machine does most of the work, the human is left with the hard leftovers plus a boring, tiring job of watching the machine. And because you practice less, you get worse at the exact moment you are needed. Forty years old, and it describes watching an AI agent work very well.

Read it: [Automatica, vol. 19, no. 6, pp. 775 to 779 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8)
Free copy: [gwern.net](https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/technology/1983-bainbridge.pdf)

## Try it this week

- Day 1: pick your three tools. Write them on a sticky note.
- Day 2: put one easy task back into your day on purpose.
- Day 3: send your next big task out, and do not check on it for an hour.
- Day 4: pick a stop time. Close the laptop at that time.
